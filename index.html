Supplementary Interactive Figure S1

Party lean and state conditions across the 50 states

Designed and authored by David A. McLeod, PhD, MSW

States are grouped into five equal groups of 10 by their average presidential vote across the seven elections from 2000 to 2024, from Strongly Democratic to Strongly Republican. Choose an outcome to compare.

How to read this page. Deeper purple means more Republican and worse off. A state's purple deepens as its party lean moves from Strongly Democratic to Strongly Republican (up the side of the legend), and deepens again as the selected outcome moves from the best 10 states to the worst 10 (across the legend). The lightest states, near white, are Strongly Democratic and among the best on that outcome. The darkest states, a deep purple close to black, are Strongly Republican and among the worst. If the two line up, the states pile up from the white corner to the dark corner of the legend. Outcomes with no agreed better or worse direction (fertility, marriage, divorce, cesarean delivery, births to unmarried mothers) shade the outcome in gray instead, from lower to higher. The number in each legend square is how many states fall there. Below the map, the dot chart shows every state's actual value on the same outcome, sorted into the five party-lean groups, with a bar marking each group's average. Hover over a state or a dot to see its numbers, or over a legend square to highlight its states. The menu holds all 97 outcomes, organized by domain; use Previous and Next to step through them. The table at the bottom gives the average for each party-lean group; click a row to map that outcome.

Note. Party-lean groups are fifths (quintiles) of the 50 states on average Republican share of all presidential votes, 2000–2024; R and D figures in the headers are group averages of Republican and Democratic shares. Group 5 (Strongly Republican): all 10 states voted Republican in all seven elections; group 1 (Strongly Democratic): all 10 voted Democratic in all seven. Groups are a display device; the manuscript estimates associations with the continuous measure. Values are unadjusted. Large, sparsely populated states take up more map area than their weight in the analysis. Outcomes cover all 97 measures in the main analysis; source abbreviations appear in each label (CDC, KCD = KIDS COUNT, GT = Georgetown, IWPR SoW, USN = U.S. News, WH = WalletHub, EW = Education Week, CAWP, ACS). Sources for every outcome, with APA references and links, are listed under Sources and references below. Associations across states do not show cause and do not describe individual voters.

Sources and references

Election data and party lean. Republican and Democratic shares for 2000–2020 come from the MIT Election Data and Science Lab (2017) and for 2024 from the American Presidency Project (n.d.), as shares of all presidential votes cast. COVID-19 deaths are cumulative deaths from The New York Times (2023) divided by April 1, 2020, Census resident population (U.S. Census Bureau, 2021). Map boundaries come from U.S. Census Bureau cartographic boundary files (2017 edition) as distributed in us-atlas (Bostock, 2023b); the page is built with D3.js (Bostock, 2023a). Where a source has been updated since, the edition used in the analysis is the one listed. Each outcome's source and data year appear in the table; the full reference list follows.
